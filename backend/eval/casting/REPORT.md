# Actor search: does it find the right actor?

Generated by `python -m eval.casting.run report`.

- **Briefs:** 52 casting briefs, each written by hand from a real part and resolved to its actor through TMDb
- **Corpus:** 1159 actors. The part each brief was drawn from is removed from that actor's record before anything is embedded
- **Model:** `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`, run locally
- **Ranked to depth 50**; anything below that counts as not found

Chance alone scores 0% at 5 and 1% at 10 on a corpus this size. Any number near that line is evidence of nothing.

**`semantic vs lexical`:** recall@10 differs by -15 points (95% interval -29 to -2, p = 0.993 that the first is not actually ahead).

**`semantic vs chance`:** recall@10 differs by +19 points (95% interval +10 to +31, p = 0.000 that the first is not actually ahead).

**`semantic_roles_first vs lexical`:** recall@10 differs by -17 points (95% interval -31 to -4, p = 0.996 that the first is not actually ahead).

**`semantic_roles_first vs chance`:** recall@10 differs by +17 points (95% interval +8 to +29, p = 0.000 that the first is not actually ahead).

**`semantic (bge-small-en-v1.5) vs lexical`:** recall@10 differs by -2 points (95% interval -17 to +13, p = 0.651 that the first is not actually ahead).

**`semantic (bge-small-en-v1.5) vs chance`:** recall@10 differs by +33 points (95% interval +21 to +46, p = 0.000 that the first is not actually ahead).

**Word overlap beats the embedding.** 19% against 35% at 10, and the interval on the gap (-29 to -2 points) stays below zero, so this is not a quirk of the sample. On this task the sentence-transformer dependency is costing accuracy rather than buying it.

## What this cannot tell you

- **A corpus of a few thousand is not a casting database.** Recall falls as the pool grows, so this is an upper bound on what the same search would do over every working actor.
- **The distractors are famous people.** They come from TMDb's popular pages, so the pool is more uniform — and probably harder to separate — than a real roster of local actors with thin credits. Which way that biases the number is not obvious, and it is not measured here.
- **One right answer per brief.** Several actors could play most of these parts well. A brief whose top five are all plausible is scored as a miss unless the one who actually got the part is among them, so recall here understates usefulness.
- **The briefs are mine.** I wrote them after knowing who played the part, and no amount of care fully removes that. They name no actor, film or character — a test enforces all three — but a subtler echo of the performance is possible.
- **Nothing here tests the age and gender filters** the product's search also applies. Ranking is measured unfiltered, and those filters could only help.
